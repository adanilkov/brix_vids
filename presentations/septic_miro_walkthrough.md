# Septic contractor Miro walkthrough

**Working title:** Why good septic companies lose jobs online — and what to fix first  
**Format:** One continuous Miro board, recorded as a 6–7 minute screen share  
**Audience:** Owner of a local septic service company  
**Goal:** Teach practical steps first, then offer BRIX Foundation as an option for the marketing work

## Board setup

Create eight 16:9 frames in one horizontal row. Use a single path across the top of the board:

**Homeowner searches → compares → contacts → books → leaves feedback**

Give each frame one large headline, no more than three short notes, and one simple visual. Use BRIX ink `#061c2b`, orange `#e85816`, paper `#f7f4ed`, and sage `#78998f`. Use the BRIX logo only on the first and last frames. Orange marks the point where a customer might be lost. Use a sample homeowner named **Maria** throughout, so the board feels like one story. Keep the source links and production notes below off camera.

## Frame 1 — The homeowner is already looking (0:00–0:40)

**On board:** `Maria needs septic service today` → phone with `septic pumping near me` → three contractor cards. Underneath: `Found? Trusted? Reached?`

**Narration:**

> Imagine Maria notices a septic problem at home. She picks up her phone and searches for help nearby. She is not studying marketing. She wants to know who handles the job, who looks trustworthy, and who will actually get back to her. A good septic company can lose that call at any of those steps. Let's walk through what you can fix yourself, starting with the easiest leak: how fast you respond.

**Recording move:** Zoom from the search box to a ringing phone on the next frame.

## Frame 2 — Answer while the job is still on their mind (0:40–1:35)

**On board:** `New inquiry` → `5-minute target` → `real conversation`. Smaller note: `Alert → acknowledgment → owner/dispatcher call`. Show a second phone labeled `another contractor` with a clock.

**Narration:**

> If Maria submits a form or leaves a voicemail, she may contact more than one company. Aim to answer a new inquiry within five minutes during your stated business hours. That is a working target, not a guarantee that every lead will book. Send the form or missed call to the person who can respond, send a short acknowledgment right away, and have a real person call or text to learn what is happening and schedule the next step. If you are on a job site, assign a backup. The first reply should say who you are and when you can actually talk. An automatic “we got your message” is useful, but it is not the same as helping the customer.

**DIY note to reveal:** `Test your own form and phone. Time the first useful response.`

## Frame 3 — Reviews help them choose (1:35–2:30)

**On board:** Two sample business cards: `4.9 ★ / 8 reviews` and `4.7 ★ / 86 reviews`. Add `Quality + quantity + recency + replies`; do not imply either sample card always wins.

**Narration:**

> Now Maria compares the companies she found. She will probably look at both the star rating and how many real customers took the time to review the work. A strong rating with only a handful of reviews can leave questions; a larger history gives her more to read. Google's own guidance says review count and positive ratings can help local ranking, and replying to reviews helps customers see that you pay attention. The way to build this is simple: ask every real customer for an honest review after the job, make it easy with your Google review link, and answer feedback professionally. Do it consistently and let the numbers build over time.

**DIY note to reveal:** `After every completed job: send one honest review request.`

## Frame 4 — Make review follow-up simple and fair (2:30–3:20)

**On board:** `Job complete` → `same request to every customer` → `Google review link`. Below the path: `Customer can also reply privately if something needs attention.` No star-score decision diamond.

**Narration:**

> You can automate that follow-up text. For example: “Thanks for choosing us. Would you share your honest experience on Google? Here's the link. If anything still needs attention, reply here and we'll help.” Send that same opportunity to everyone, whether you think they loved the job or not. Some systems ask for a rating first and send only happy customers to Google. Skip that setup. It hides critical feedback from the public review path, and Google does not allow selectively asking for positive reviews. If someone reports a problem, respond and make it right. Their ability to leave an honest public review should stay open.

**DIY note to reveal:** `Copy: “Thanks for choosing [Company]. Would you share your honest experience? [Google link] If anything needs attention, reply here.”`

## Frame 5 — Keep the Google profile useful (3:20–4:05)

**On board:** A Google Business Profile checklist: `Correct phone, hours, services, service area` / `Current job photos` / `Review replies`. Draw an arrow from the profile to both `Call` and `Website`.

**Narration:**

> Your Google Business Profile is often Maria's first look at your company. Claim and verify it if you have not already. Check the phone number, business hours, service area, and the septic services you actually offer. Add clear photos of real jobs and respond to reviews. A useful profile helps a homeowner understand what you do and how to reach you. Google says local results depend mainly on relevance, distance, and prominence, so no one can promise that a few posts or photos will put you at number one. The goal is an accurate, credible profile that gives the customer a reason to contact you.

**DIY note to reveal:** `Put a recurring 15-minute profile check on the calendar.`

## Frame 6 — Show the work, then make contacting you easy (4:05–5:05)

**On board:** Three real job photos labeled `Problem` / `Work` / `Result` → simple website wireframe with `Services`, `Service area`, `Proof`, and a large `Call / Request service` button.

**Narration:**

> The best proof is work you have actually done. Take a few clear, permission-approved photos on each job: what the issue was, what your crew did, and what the finished result looked like. Put them on your profile and on your website. A clean site gives you room to explain pumping, inspections, repairs, and the areas you serve. It should load well on a phone and make the next step obvious: call or request service. Helpful pages can also give Google more to understand about your business in ordinary search. Your website can be linked from your Business Profile, so the two work together. A website alone does not guarantee higher rankings or more calls, but it can make a good first impression easier to act on.

**DIY note to reveal:** `For each core service: one clear page, real photos, and one obvious way to contact you.`

## Frame 7 — The do-it-yourself plan (5:05–5:55)

**On board:** Four stacked cards labeled `This week`, `After every job`, `Each week`, `Each month`.

- **This week:** Test your contact form and missed-call process; update profile info and website contact buttons.
- **After every job:** Capture approved photos; ask every customer for an honest review.
- **Each week:** Add useful job photos or updates; reply to reviews; check missed inquiries.
- **Each month:** Review calls, inquiries, booked estimates, and which pages or services brought them in.

**Narration:**

> You can do this without an agency. Start this week by testing your phone and form and updating your profile. After each job, save photos and send an honest review request. Every week, check the profile and reply to reviews. Every month, look beyond views and clicks: how many inquiries came in, how many became estimates, and how many became jobs? The work is straightforward, but it has to happen while you are also running crews and serving customers.

## Recording notes

- Speak as if explaining the board to one owner, not reading a slide deck. Pause briefly after each reveal.
- Pan to the next frame only after finishing the current idea. Zoom close enough for phone viewers to read the few words on screen.
- Use sample review counts and search results as illustrations, not claims about a real competitor.
- Keep the BRIX plan details exactly aligned with the live pricing page when recording. As checked September 30, 2026, Foundation is $297/month and includes automatic **review requests**; automatic **new-lead follow-up** is listed under Growth.
- Use the five-minute goal as an operating target. The older lead-response research concerns lead qualification across businesses; it does not prove a septic contractor will get a particular conversion rate.
